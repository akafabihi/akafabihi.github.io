My research interests lie in cancer metabolism, immunometabolism, and cancer immunology, and more broadly in how cells adapt their metabolism under stress and how those changes shape cancer and the immune response. The interests below are grouped by domain, and my upcoming PhD work on Spatial-SCENITH comes first.

## Spatial & Functional Immunometabolism

Bulk and single-cell profiling tell us what a cell is *capable* of, because the presence of an enzyme, transporter, or transcript only shows the machinery. They do not show metabolic *flux*, which is the set of pathways a cell is actually running. This gap grows wider in stressed, dysfunctional cells, where a hostile microenvironment can shut a pathway down even while its machinery stays fully expressed. I am especially interested in closing that gap at single-cell resolution, in intact tissue.

### Spatial-SCENITH: functional metabolic profiling at single-cell resolution, in situ

This is the focus of my PhD with [Dr. Rafael J. Argüello](https://ciml.univ-mrs.fr/fr/member/rafael-jose-arguello/) at CIML, within the [UNLIMITED](https://www.unimedizin-mainz.de/unlimited-msca/home.html) MSCA doctoral network. SCENITH (Argüello et al., 2020) measures metabolic dependencies by using puromycin incorporation as a proxy for protein translation, and therefore for bioenergetic flux, after treating cells with targeted metabolic inhibitors. I want to give this functional readout spatial and single-cell resolution: treating live tissue slices, capturing the SCENITH response by cyclic imaging, and measuring which metabolic pathway each cell is using *while the cell stays where it sits in the tissue*. My longer-term goal is an integrated atlas of glycolytic-stress-driven metabolic states across inflammation, infection, and cancer.

### Mapping the spatial progression of metabolic exhaustion in CD8+ T cells

T-cell exhaustion is not a single state but a process that runs from progenitor through intermediate to terminally exhausted cells, and recent work suggests that metabolic collapse is a *driver* of that process rather than a downstream consequence. Because these drivers (hypoxia, nutrient scarcity, suppressive metabolites, and niche support from dendritic cells) are spatially organized along gradients that run from the vasculature into the tumor core, I expect the dysfunction to be spatially structured too. I want to use Spatial-SCENITH to map the flux states of tumor-infiltrating CD8+ T cells along those gradients, in intact tumor, and to test for *capability-versus-flux decoupling*: terminal cells that still carry the metabolic machinery (for example, fatty-acid-oxidation transcripts) but have lost the corresponding flux. A later experiment would directly change a mitochondrial regulator (PGC-1α) to test whether the metabolic axis is a cause of the exhaustion process, not just correlated with it.

## Cancer Metabolism

### Metabolic reprogramming, plasticity, and adaptation

I am interested in how cancer cells quickly rewire their metabolic networks to survive nutrient scarcity, hypoxia, and drug treatment. This flexibility is a survival advantage for the tumor: a treatment that works in a nutrient-rich region can still fail in a hypoxic, nutrient-poor core. I want to model this behavior to find tumor-specific vulnerabilities that *persist* despite metabolic adaptation, thereby exploiting the cancer's own flexibility against itself.

### Spatial metabolic heterogeneity in the tumor microenvironment

My MSc thesis examined metabolic heterogeneity in pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors (PanNETs), where I identified the lactate transporters MCT1 and MCT4 as functionally redundant exporters (co-targeting both works better than single-target inhibition under stress), and the acetyl-CoA pathway (ACSS2) as a vulnerability under nutrient deprivation. I built 2D, spheroid, and patient-derived tumoroid models to reproduce intratumoral heterogeneity under hypoxia and nutrient deprivation. I am increasingly interested in computational models that account for this spatial heterogeneity to predict metabolic "escape routes" before they become clinical treatment failure, alongside spatially resolved readouts such as DESI and MALDI mass spectrometry imaging.

## Immuno-oncology

### Metabolic crosstalk between cancer cells and tumor-infiltrating immune cells

Reprogramming of cancer cell metabolism also shapes the surrounding immune cells. As tumour cells undergo the Warburg effect and export lactate via MCT1 and MCT4, lactate and metabolic waste build up, polarising tumour-associated macrophages (TAMs) towards an immunosuppressive M2-like phenotype and reducing the cytotoxicity of effector T and NK cells. I am interested in exploiting these metabolic shifts to relieve immune suppression and improve the response to immunotherapy, for instance by lowering microenvironmental lactate through MCT inhibition.

### Targeting tumor-associated macrophage metabolism

TAMs carry their own metabolic alterations, including itaconate (ITA) accumulation downstream of ACOD1, which is associated with reduced CD8+ T-cell infiltration. I am interested in engineering human iPSC-derived CAR macrophages (CAR iMACs) that lack ACOD1 to lower ITA and its suppressive effects, and in measuring the effects on T-cell infiltration and immune-cell metabolism with single-cell RNA-seq from tumor tissue and tumor-draining lymph nodes.

### Exercise Physiology

I am an endurance athlete (trail running and cycling), and I treat my own training as an ongoing experiment: testing new training regimes, trying different fueling strategies, and optimizing my own performance as an n-of-1. I also read the research on endurance performance, training zones, and how effective different training interventions really are. My focus is on athletic performance rather than general-population health. I am chasing the outlier, not the average, which is part of why I am drawn to Kilian Jornet's approach to training and to the limits of human endurance. The bioenergetics I study in cells also connect, at a different scale, to what happens in a working muscle.

## Philosophical Interest

Outside the lab, philosophy is my long-running intellectual hobby. I have written about it for years, mostly on my [blog](https://fabifuu.wordpress.com/category/filsafat/), from the basic question of [what philosophy and a definition even are](https://fabifuu.wordpress.com/2018/03/02/sebelum-menjawab-apa-itu-filsafat-kita-perlu-menjawab-apa-itu-sebuah-pengantar-mengenai-definisi-dari-definisi/) to the place of [philosophy in education and society](https://fabifuu.wordpress.com/2017/02/11/pendidikan-masyarakat-dan-filsafat/). A few of the areas I keep coming back to:

### Epistemology

I am drawn most to epistemology, which is really just the [study of knowledge itself](https://fabifuu.wordpress.com/2019/03/20/pengantar-ke-dalam-masalah-masalah-epistemologi/). I keep returning to one simple-sounding question: what does it actually mean to *know* something, rather than to believe it and happen to be right? From there it opens up into how we justify our beliefs, whether knowledge comes more from reason or from experience, and how far knowledge can reach at all (Gödel's incompleteness theorem is a humbling answer to that last one). I have also written about [Kant's account of knowledge](https://fabifuu.wordpress.com/2019/03/20/kant/) and about how [G. E. Moore tried to answer the skeptic](https://fabifuu.wordpress.com/2017/06/12/moore-skeptisisme-dan-eksternalisme-semantik/) who doubts the outside world is even there.

### Philosophy of Language and Truth

I am also fascinated by language and truth. It still amazes me that words manage to latch onto the world at all, and that we can say something true or false about things out there. I have written about the [correspondence theory of truth](https://fabifuu.wordpress.com/2019/03/06/correspondence-theory-of-truth-and-facts-introduction-part-i/), the idea that a statement is true when it matches the way things really are, along with the [relationship between words and the things they name](https://fabifuu.wordpress.com/2019/03/06/lebih-suci-mana-kata-atau-benda-metafisika-eksistensi-dan-filsafat-bahasa-dalam-balutan-diskusi-santuy/), and how the meaning of a word can depend on the world outside our heads as much as on what is in them.

### Philosophy of Science and Biology

As a scientist, I think a lot about how science actually works. How is a single piece of evidence supposed to confirm a whole theory (the [raven paradox](https://fabifuu.wordpress.com/2017/03/29/paradoks-gagak/) shows how strange that question can get), and why does our entire picture of the world sometimes shift all at once? I am especially interested in the philosophy of biology, since biology is my own field, and in the history and assumptions behind it. I once wrote about how today's [biologists are, in a way, the heirs of the old alchemists](https://fabifuu.wordpress.com/2019/01/20/biologists-are-the-spiritual-successor-of-the-alchemists/), chasing many of the same questions about life with far better tools, and elsewhere about [reason, human exceptionalism, and evolution](https://fabifuu.wordpress.com/2016/11/05/tentang-akal-sauvinisme-manusia-dan-evolusi/).

### Existentialism and the Absurd

On the more personal side, I keep returning to Camus and his question of how to live once you accept that the universe hands us no built-in meaning. I read his absurdism as hopeful rather than bleak: instead of giving up, we can keep going and even find some joy in it. That, to me, is the point of his famous line that we should imagine Sisyphus happy. In a similar spirit, I have written about [belief and the contemporary "gods" we still build for ourselves](https://fabifuu.wordpress.com/2016/10/31/kepercayaan-dan-tuhan-tuhan-kontemporer/).

### Philosophy through Film and Anime

Finally, I like meeting these ideas in stories as much as in textbooks. I have written about Camus through the anime [*Girls' Last Tour*](https://fabifuu.wordpress.com/2019/06/28/girls-last-tour-dan-albert-camus/), about [*Tokyo Sonata*](https://fabifuu.wordpress.com/2019/01/08/tokyo-sonata-sebuah-refleksi-kehidupan-masyarakat-modern-jepang/) as a quiet reflection on modern life, and about dreams and simulated reality through Satoshi Kon's [*Paprika*](https://fabifuu.wordpress.com/2016/10/01/mimpi-paprika-dan-realita-tersimulasi/). For me, film and anime are one of the most natural ways to think a philosophical idea all the way through.
