I am Abdulloh Kafa Bihi, and you can call me Fabi. I am a biologist, currently working as a Senior Biocurator at NalaGenetics, where I works on pharmacogenomics, disease risk prediction, and wellness genomics. In September 2026, I will join CIML / Aix-Marseille Université to pursue my PhD in cancer metabolism and spatial immunometabolism.

My primary research interest are understanding [how cancer cells reprogram their metabolism](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41568-021-00378-6) to survive nutrient scarcity, hypoxia, and therapeutic stress, and how that same reprogramming [reshapes the tumor microenvironment into an immunosuppressive](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41590-023-01675-y) one. As tumor cells adapt, they deplete oxygen and accumulate metabolic waste such as lactate, which impairs cytotoxic T cell and NK cell function while polarizing tumor-associated macrophages toward pro-tumor phenotypes. Metabolic reprogramming, therefore, acts as both survival strategy (to fund the massive biosynthesis requirement of growing tumor), and mechanism of immune evasion!

Much of our current understanding is based on bulk or single-cell profiling, which identifies the transcripts and proteins expressed by a cell. However, the presence of an enzyme or transporter only indicates what a cell is capable of doing. It does not reveal which pathways the cell is actually utilizing. This gap grows wider in stressed, dysfunctional cells, where a hostile microenvironment can shut down a pathway even if the machinery for it (i.e., enzyme, mRNA, transporters) is fully expressed. Furthermore, dissociation of tissue also discards spatial context, such as a cell's position relative to the vasculature and hypoxic core, which is itself a determinant of its metabolic state. My future work is based on the premise that metabolic state must be measured functionally and in situ at the single-cell level in intact tissue.

## Short Biography

Starting September 2026, I will join the lab of [Dr. Rafael J. Argüello](https://ciml.univ-mrs.fr/fr/member/rafael-jose-arguello/) at the **Centre d'Immunologie de Marseille-Luminy (CIML)** as a Marie Skłodowska-Curie (MSCA) Doctoral Candidate in the [UNLIMITED](https://www.unimedizin-mainz.de/unlimited-msca/home.html) doctoral network. My project, *Spatial-SCENITH*, aims to add spatial and single-cell resolution to functional immunometabolic profiling, building the first integrated atlas of glycolytic-stress-driven metabolic states across inflammation, infection, and cancer.

Before this, I completed my MSc in Molecular Life Sciences at the **University of Bern**, where my thesis examined metabolic heterogeneity in pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors (PanNETs). I developed 2D, spheroid, and patient-derived tumoroid models to recapitulate intratumoral metabolic heterogeneity under hypoxia and nutrient deprivation. Combining RNA-seq, Seahorse metabolic flux analysis, time-lapse microscopy, and quantitative single-cell confocal imaging, I showed that the lactate transporters MCT1 and MCT4 act as functionally redundant exporters, and that co-targeting both outperforms single-target inhibition under environmental stress. I also identified the acetyl-CoA pathway as a vulnerability under nutrient deprivation. This work appears as a preprint (bioRxiv 2025) and convinced me that functional metabolic profiling only becomes meaningful at spatial and single-cell resolution.

I currently work as a **Senior Biocurator (Lead) at NalaGenetics** in Singapore. I lead the curation effort for IAF-ICP, an A\*STAR-funded grant developing long-read sequencing and combined risk-prediction models for cancer, cardiovascular, and neurodegenerative disease, and I contribute to APEX, a psychiatric pharmacogenomics project on treatment-resistant schizophrenia. Alongside this, I build and maintain curated databases that integrate polygenic, monogenic, and clinical risk for personalized medicine.

My approach is deliberately hybrid: wet-lab metabolic phenotyping and 3D imaging on one side, and reproducible computational analysis (R, Python, RNA-seq, image segmentation) on the other. I believe the most interesting biology lives where those two skill sets meet.
